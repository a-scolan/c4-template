Yes — this is absolutely an ADR-worthy architecture decision because it changes a core integration boundary and the shape of the payment architecture. A good framing, following `projects/template/ADR/0000-template.md`, is: Title: Replace the internal payment orchestration service with direct Stripe integration behind a thin internal adapter. Status: Proposed. Context: the current internal orchestration layer adds latency, ownership cost, and complexity; the business primarily needs capabilities already provided by Stripe; the team still wants an internal boundary so the domain model is not tightly coupled to vendor SDK details, webhook formats, or error semantics. Decision: remove the internal orchestration service as the main payment path; integrate directly with Stripe; keep a small internal adapter or anti-corruption layer that owns request mapping, webhook translation, idempotency handling, error normalization, secrets/configuration, and observability. Consequences — Positive: fewer hops, lower maintenance, faster feature delivery, clearer ownership, smaller failure surface, better access to native Stripe features. Negative: stronger vendor coupling, migration effort, webhook and retry complexity moves closer to the product team, future multi-provider support becomes harder. Neutral: the thin adapter becomes the intentional seam for future change, but not a full orchestration platform. Notes: capture alternatives such as keeping the current service or building a broader PSP abstraction, and include migration, rollback, compliance, and webhook security considerations.
