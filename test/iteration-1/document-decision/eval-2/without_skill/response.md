Yes. This is a real ADR because it changes system boundaries, ownership, operational risk, and vendor dependency. Suggested title: Replace the internal payment orchestration service with a direct Stripe integration behind a thin internal adapter. Context: explain why the current orchestration layer is being removed, what complexity or latency it adds, which payment capabilities are truly needed, and the business drivers such as speed, reliability, cost, or maintainability. Decision: integrate directly with Stripe from the relevant service or services while preserving a small internal adapter boundary for domain isolation, webhook handling, idempotency, and future replaceability. Consequences: pros include less internal complexity, faster feature delivery, and use of Stripe-managed capabilities; cons include tighter vendor coupling, Stripe-specific failure modes, API limit concerns, webhook/retry design work, compliance considerations, and a clearer exit strategy requirement. Alternatives to record: keep the internal orchestrator, adopt another PSP abstraction layer, or use a hybrid model. Also capture guardrails for what remains behind the adapter and what would trigger revisiting the decision.
